Running the risers in oak rather than painting them white is a quieter, warmer
look than the usual contrast, and it puts the emphasis on the timber itself.
It also raises the standard of the work: with no paint to cover the joint
between tread and riser, every one has to close properly on its own.

The ironwork is mostly plain square bar, with a rectangular insert repeating at
intervals rather than on every baluster. Spacing that repeat evenly across a
flight that includes a winder turn takes setting out before anything is cut —
the turn eats into the run, and a pattern that is not planned around it ends up
crowded exactly where the eye lands.

The newel posts were made square with a shallow groove worked into the faces,
picking up the lines of the balusters without adding another material.
