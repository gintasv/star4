Under the carpet on most builder-built staircases is not a floor. It is
construction-grade timber that was never meant to be looked at — rough, marked
with staples, and often a different species tread to tread, because it was
always going to be covered.

So this is not a refinishing job, whatever it looks like from the top of the
stairs. The old treads come off and new solid oak goes on, with the risers
made good and painted. What makes it worth doing is that the structure
underneath is usually sound, so you are paying for the visible surface rather
than a whole new staircase.

The risers go on painted, which is the detail that makes the finished flight
read as deliberate rather than as a repair. A crisp white riser against a clean
oak nosing is what the eye reads as a new staircase, even though the structure
underneath has not moved.

Worth knowing before you start: the transformation is dramatic but the house is
disrupted while it happens. This is the only route between floors, so the work
is staged to leave a safe way up and down at the end of each day.
