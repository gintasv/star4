A vaulted room with a skylight is the hardest place to lay a floor, because it
is the hardest place to hide anything. Direct overhead light rakes across the
boards and picks up any cupping, any lipping between planks, and any run that
drifts out of parallel with the walls.

So the sequence matters more than the speed. The subfloor was flattened and
moisture-tested before the material came in, and the boards were left in the
room to acclimatise — wide planks move more than narrow ones, and a floor laid
before it has settled to the room will show it at the seams within a season.

The setting-out ran off the longest sightline rather than off a wall, which is
what keeps the boards reading straight down the length of the space. Around the
fireplace the boards were scribed to the brick rather than covered with a
trim strip, so the floor meets the stone directly.
