High contrast is unforgiving. Dark treads against white risers means every
joint, every reveal and every slightly proud nosing is visible from across the
room, because there is nowhere for a discrepancy to hide.

That is most of the work on a stair like this: getting the rise consistent
tread to tread, keeping the skirt line clean where it meets the wall, and
making sure the bullnose bottom step is square to the floor rather than to the
wall. The stain was built up in coats and checked under the room's own lighting
between each one — espresso reads very differently in daylight than it does
under warm downlights, and the only place that judgement can be made is on
site.

The railing carries the same detail around the upper landing so the two levels
read as one piece of work.
